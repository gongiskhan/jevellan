**Evidence label: simulated.** Projects phase 5 (BRIEF-projects.md section 13, local and untracked like BRIEF.md) passes PJ5 on 2026-10-05 (goncalos-macbook-pro) as one integration test with a simulated member device, beside the remote-thread, move and relay tests and the first Call B case on a booted daemon, and as two Playwright browser journeys in four layouts (desktop and phone, light and dark). **Both devices are daemons on this one machine**, talking over local HTTP; no second physical device was used (`JEVELLAN_TEST_SECOND_DEVICE` is not set, DEVICE), as brief section 13 allows for PJ5 ("simulated member device as in J8/J11"). **Every Jev answer is simulated**, **live Jev was not run** (`JEVELLAN_TEST_JEV_KEY` is not set, ENV-JEV), and **the automated vision checks were not run** (`JEVELLAN_TEST_CLAUDE_TOKEN` is not set, ENV-CLAUDE), so every PJ5 vision record says `not run`. A builder visual review of the screenshots (below) found and fixed problems but does not stand for the vision checks. No part of this evidence is live, and Gonçalo's acceptance is not claimed.

- **Real:** two `Application` daemons, the fixture's hub and a member joined to it with a real invite (its own home, device token, browser session and heartbeat), with their HTTP routes, the owner proxy and the peer receiver; the hub's SQLite project store, including the new envelope relay; each device's outbox and inbox files, ledgers and coordinator queue; Git (the project checkout and a clone on the member, thread worktrees and `jv/` branches in each device's own home, a bare origin); process groups; a real restart of the hub. In the browser journeys: Playwright's Chromium driving the compiled web application against the `scripts/test-server.mjs --projects` fixture server's hub and member daemons.

- **Not run:** the vision checks (ENV-CLAUDE), live Jev (ENV-JEV), live Claude and Codex turns, live GitHub, a second physical device (DEVICE) and PJ-live (phase 8).

## The Call B case

## Integration tests

Every case boots real daemons through the shared project fixture; the member cases add a simulated member joined over HTTP (decision 500). Each ends with the fixture's leak check, and PJ5 also checks that the member's native session ids, bridge tokens, worktree paths and browser session appear in no hub response, view, index, question, ledger or relay row.

- `tests/projects-mesh.test.ts` (new, 1 test): `PJ5 the hub coordinator runs a thread on a member through the relay`, in five steps: the start and placement, the report, the proxied page and actions with the peer receiver checks, the hub outage, and the move followed by the hub's restart.
- `tests/projects-remote.test.ts` (new, 3 tests): `a thread placed on a member starts there with its account, counts until the member publishes it, reports through the relay, and its page and actions are proxied from the hub`; `from the member, coordinator requests and its chat reach the hub coordinator, New thread places there, lists cost one hub read, and a restart of a member thread starts through the hub`; `the coordinator acts on member threads through commands, reads them with the transcript left there, follows their pull requests from GitHub, and starts queued member threads from its sweep`.
- `tests/projects-move.test.ts` (new, 4 tests): `the move rule: a device that is gone, revoked or offline, or whose own status says it runs no turn, gives the coordinator up`; `Move coordinator here is refused while the coordinator works and allowed once it is idle; the new device starts fresh from the hub notebook and indexes, postpones while the hub is unreachable, and the old device never runs a coordinator turn again`; `an offline coordinator device: the hub takes the coordinator over, events relayed to the gone device follow the move, and the device that returns stops its turn and hands its queue over`; `a coordinator that failed twice gives the coordinator up: its waiting message reaches the new device at its next sweep`.
- `tests/project-envelopes.test.ts` (new, 10 tests): sequence order per project and relay order at the target; idempotency at the outbox, the hub and the inbox across a lost put reply, a lost acknowledgement and a restart; the newest 2,000 seen ids; the retry schedule of 10, 20, 40, 60 and 60 seconds; the hub down and up over HTTP with the coordinator resolved when sent; refusals dropped while a failing envelope holds only its own sequence; the hub's source and target rules, deletion on acknowledgement and pages by count and size; the move's retargeting; members over HTTP with their device token; a thread command applied once per command id.
- Also extended: `tests/project-schemas.test.ts` 71 to 78, `tests/project-work-ui.test.ts` 31 to 33 (the start window and the offline-on-open page), `tests/owner-proxy.test.ts` 7 to 8, `tests/project-admission.test.ts` 7 to 8 and `tests/project-hub-store.test.ts` 10 to 11, and `tests/lifecycle.test.ts` 4 to 6 for the pre-existing request defect the phase gate found (decision 510).

## Limits

- Everything above is simulated: both devices run on this one machine, and there is no live Jev answer, no live Claude or Codex turn and no real GitHub repository or pull request. PJ-live (phase 8) runs the live journey or records it as blocked.
- The vision checks were not run, so no screenshot has a vision judgment. The builder review is a single pass by the builder, not the vision checks brief section 13 asks for.
- Brief section 4 decision 7 is not built for an offline coordinator device: a thread's needs-decision report waits until a coordinator runs instead of becoming an owner question directly. Move coordinator here is the way out. Update (2026-10-05, after the phase 5 gate): built; while the coordinator's device is away, the device that owns the thread asks the owner directly (decision 512).
- Jev choosing the member device and the former coordinator device running no turn are proven by the integration tests only; in the browser the owner fixes the device, and the moved coordinator's former device has no daemon. The hub outage is simulated from the member's side; the offline-on-open thread page is checked in the browser with a stubbed 409.
- A hub outage before a task turn, a verification retry or a publication still ends that step as a failure that nothing resumes, and a launch whose own hub reads fail after the turn's state change ends as a failed turn; only a turn for waiting messages rests on the hub (decision 505). A member's browser cannot act while the hub is unreachable (the existing sign-in rule).
- The New thread and Override device selects offer every available device; one without the project path or an eligible account is refused after Apply, in the dialog. Update (2026-10-05, after the phase 5 gate): both list every device of the mesh and disable those no thread of the project can run on, with the reason (decision 513).
- A move accepted between the coordinator's last assignment check and its launch lets that one turn run on the former device. Move does not check that this device can run the coordinator; it then reads `Unavailable` here and can be moved again. The record of questions asked directly while the coordinator could not run does not travel with forwarded events. Update (2026-10-05, after the phase 5 gate): all three are closed, with the new limits listed in REPORT.md: the hub orders a move against every announced turn (decision 515), Move first checks that this device can run the coordinator (decision 514), and the coordinator reads such a report as already asked from the project's question list (decision 512).
- Thread starts and commands for a device revoked later stay on the hub (only coordinator events follow a move), and a queued start sent to a device that never publishes it keeps its project's queue sweep reading the hub.
- A new remote thread's page waits for its start for two minutes by the creation time in its id, which assumes device clocks agree within that. A thread opened while its device is offline shows the last state of its index row, so a device that slept mid-turn reads `Running` beside the notice.
- The fixture member polls the hub every 500 ms on the `--projects` servers. The tablet widths (761 to 1180 px) are covered only by hand checks, and the device switcher still overflows the sidebar at 761 to 1024 px (pre-existing).
